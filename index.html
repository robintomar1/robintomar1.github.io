About

Resume ↗

Over the past six years, I've worked across the full stack of robotics systems — from low-level hardware control to high-level behavior and manipulation. I started my journey working on autonomous mobile robots, focusing on navigation, control, and large-scale deployments internationally. That experience shaped my approach to engineering: practical, detail-oriented, and rooted in real-world constraints.

More recently, my work has centered around robot manipulation — designing and building systems that can interact with messy, unstructured environments. At Clutterbot, I've been leading the development of the core manipulation stack, where I've worked on everything from architecture design to hardware validation. I've helped evolve the system from deterministic control pipelines to more adaptive approaches using motion planning and imitation learning.

A big part of my work involves bridging gaps — between software, hardware, research and product. I've led initiatives like redesigning core system architectures, integrating frameworks like MoveIt, and exploring learning-based approaches for manipulation. I've also driven cross-team efforts during critical milestones.

Beyond individual contributions, I care deeply about building strong engineering teams. I've mentored engineers, led technical discussions, and pushed for better documentation and system design practices. I enjoy breaking down complex problems, helping others grow, and creating an environment where good engineering decisions can thrive.

What excites me most is the possibility of what robots can achieve given all monumental interest from everyone in the field today. I'm particularly interested in working at the cutting edge of robotics manipulation and control with methods which can scale beyond controlled environments.

At the core, I'm someone who takes ownership, values clarity, and is willing to go the extra mile to make things work — especially when it matters most.
