LeRobot Hackathon

LeRobotHugging FaceImitation LearningHackathon

The Event

Participated in the Hugging Face LeRobot hackathon — a hands-on event focused on building and training low-cost manipulation policies using the LeRobot framework. The hackathon brought together robotics engineers and ML practitioners to push the boundaries of accessible, open-source robot learning.

What I Worked On

  • Set up a low-cost manipulation hardware platform compatible with the LeRobot stack
  • Collected teleoperation demonstration data for household manipulation tasks
  • Trained imitation learning policies using ACT (Action Chunking with Transformers) and diffusion policy architectures
  • Iterated on data collection strategies — discovering how demonstration quality and diversity directly impact policy robustness
  • Deployed trained policies back to hardware and evaluated real-world success rates

Key Learnings

  • The gap between "policy works in evaluation" and "policy works on hardware" is still significant — calibration, latency, and observation noise matter enormously
  • Data quality beats data quantity for manipulation tasks — 50 clean demonstrations outperform 200 noisy ones
  • The LeRobot framework dramatically lowers the barrier to entry for robot learning experimentation
  • Cross-pollination between the Hugging Face ML community and robotics engineers produces surprisingly practical insights

Takeaway

The hackathon reinforced a conviction I hold from my day job: the future of manipulation lies at the intersection of solid systems engineering and learned behavior. The teams that will win aren't the ones with the best models — they're the ones who can reliably get those models onto real hardware, collecting real data, in real environments.
